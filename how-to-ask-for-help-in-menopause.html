How to ask for help in menopause — without feeling like a burden

You’re the one who remembers everyone’s appointments, carries the mental load, holds it all together. So asking for help can feel like admitting defeat. It isn’t. It’s how strong people keep standing. Here’s how to ask — clearly, without apologising for existing.

A quick note: this is general, educational information, not medical advice. Decisions about HRT, medication and your health should be made with your own prescriber — always follow their guidance and ask your pharmacist if you’re unsure.

Why it feels so hard

Many women reach midlife having spent decades as the giver, not the receiver. Add perimenopausal low mood and anxiety, which quietly whisper that you’re a nuisance, and asking for help can feel almost physically impossible. Name that for what it is: not the truth, but a symptom talking.

Be specific, not brave

Vague asks are easy to miss. Specific asks get met. “I need you to take over the school run on Tuesdays” is easier to say yes to than “I need more support.” Before any conversation, decide the one or two concrete things that would actually lighten the load, and ask for those.

Asking your GP

You have limited minutes, so lead with impact. Try: “These symptoms are affecting my work, my sleep and my relationships. I’d like to talk about my options.” Hand over your written record. If HRT comes up, ask what to expect and how you’ll know it’s working. You are allowed to ask questions and to book a follow-up.

Asking your partner and family

People can’t read your mind, and resentment grows in the gap between what you need and what you’ve said out loud. Pick a calm moment, not mid-argument. Try: “I’m going through something real with my hormones. It’s not about you. Here’s what would genuinely help me right now.” Then let them help, even imperfectly.

Asking at work

You don’t have to disclose everything. You can simply request what helps: a fan, flexibility on heavy-symptom days, fewer early meetings when sleep is wrecked. Many employers now have menopause policies; it’s worth asking HR. Framing it around staying effective, not struggling, often makes it easier for both sides.

Through all of it, keep your own record. When you can point to “this is how often it happens, this is what helps,” you’re not asking people to take your word on a hard day — you’re showing them. That makes help easier to give, and easier to accept.

Common questions

How do I ask for help without feeling guilty?

Remember that asking is not failing — and some of the guilt is perimenopausal low mood talking, not fact. Keep requests specific and small, and notice that most people are relieved to be told exactly how to help.

What adjustments can I ask for at work during menopause?

Common ones include a desk fan or cooler space, flexibility on days when sleep or symptoms are bad, breaks, and understanding around brain fog. Many workplaces now have a menopause policy — it’s worth asking HR.

How do I talk to my partner about menopause?

Choose a calm moment, explain it’s physical and real, and be specific about what would help. Sharing a simple record of your symptoms can help them understand it isn’t ‘all in your head’.
