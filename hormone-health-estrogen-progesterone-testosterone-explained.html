Hormone health: estrogen, progesterone and testosterone explained

For something that runs so much of how we feel, hormones are rarely explained in plain English. If you’ve nodded along to “oestrogen, progesterone, testosterone” without really knowing what each does, this is for you — clear, calm, and genuinely useful.

A quick note: this is general, educational information, not medical advice. Decisions about HRT, medication and your health should be made with your own prescriber — always follow their guidance and ask your pharmacist if you’re unsure.

Why understanding helps

You don’t need a biology degree, but a basic grasp of your own hormones is quietly powerful. It helps you make sense of symptoms that otherwise feel random, understand what HRT is actually replacing, and ask better questions. Knowledge turns a frightening, mysterious process into something you can work with.

Oestrogen: the big player

Oestrogen (spelled “estrogen” in the US) does far more than govern periods. It influences temperature control, mood, memory, skin, bones, the heart and the genitourinary system. As it falls and swings through perimenopause, that wide reach is exactly why symptoms show up all over the body. In HRT, oestrogen is usually the main component.

Progesterone: balance and calm

If you have a womb and take oestrogen, you’re usually given progesterone (or a progestogen) too, to protect the womb lining. Many women also find it has a calming effect and can help with sleep, which is why it’s often taken at night. It’s the partner that keeps oestrogen balanced.

Testosterone: not just for men

Women make testosterone too, and it plays a role in energy, mood, concentration and libido. Levels decline with age, and some women are prescribed it — where appropriate and under specialist guidance — particularly for low libido that hasn’t responded to standard HRT. It’s an increasingly recognised piece of the picture.

Understanding your own pattern

General knowledge is the start; your own pattern is the point. The way these hormones affect you — which symptoms, when, and what eases them — is unique. Tracking your symptoms, cycle, treatments and daily life turns the textbook into something personal: a map of how your own body works.

Common questions

What do estrogen, progesterone and testosterone do?

Oestrogen affects temperature, mood, memory, skin, bones and more; progesterone balances oestrogen, protects the womb lining and can aid sleep; testosterone supports energy, mood, focus and libido. All three can change through menopause.

Why is progesterone given with estrogen in HRT?

If you have a womb, progesterone (or a progestogen) is usually given alongside oestrogen to protect the womb lining. Many women also find it calming and helpful for sleep, which is why it’s often taken at night.

Can women be prescribed testosterone for menopause?

Yes — some women are prescribed testosterone, usually under specialist guidance, particularly for low libido that hasn’t responded to standard HRT. It’s an individual decision with your clinician.
