Finding your HRT dose: why the journey takes patience

You hoped the first prescription would be the answer. Sometimes it is. Often it’s the start of a conversation. Finding your HRT dose is less a single decision than a gentle process of tuning — and patience, with good notes, gets you there sooner.

A quick note: this is general, educational information, not medical advice. Decisions about HRT, medication and your health should be made with your own prescriber — always follow their guidance and ask your pharmacist if you’re unsure.

There’s no one-size dose

Women absorb and respond to hormones differently, so the dose that transforms one person barely touches another. The “right” dose is simply the lowest that controls your symptoms and feels good to you — and finding it is normal, expected tuning, not a sign anything’s gone wrong.

Why it takes time

Each change needs weeks to settle before you can judge it, because your body has to reach a new steady state. That’s frustrating when you want to feel better now, but rushing leads to chopping and changing that tells you nothing. Patience here is a strategy, not just a virtue.

What a change can feel like

After an increase you might notice symptoms easing — or a few temporary adjustment effects as your body settles. After a change of type (say tablets to patches, or adding testosterone where appropriate) the picture can shift again. None of this is predictable from the outside, which is why your own notes matter more than anyone’s average.

Record each change

This is where tracking earns its place. Note the date you changed, what changed, and how you felt in the weeks after — symptoms, sleep, mood, energy, plus supplements and lifestyle. Then each adjustment builds on real evidence instead of a hazy “I think that was a bit better.” You stop going in circles.

Work with your prescriber

Dose changes should be made with your prescriber, never alone. But you are the one living in the data between appointments. When you arrive with a clear log — this dose, these weeks, this response — you become a partner in the decision, and you reach your right dose faster.

Common questions

How long does it take to find the right HRT dose?

It varies and often takes a few adjustments, with each change needing several weeks to settle before you judge it. Tracking how you respond to each dose helps you and your prescriber get there faster.

How do I know if my HRT dose is too low?

Ongoing symptoms — like continued flushes, poor sleep or low mood after things have had time to settle — can suggest the dose or type needs reviewing. Bring a record of your symptoms to your prescriber rather than changing it yourself.

Can I change my own HRT dose?

No — dose changes should be made with your prescriber. What you can do is keep a clear record of your symptoms and responses so the adjustment is based on real evidence.
