A year on HRT: what changed when I started writing it down

A year is long enough to forget how bad it was. That’s both a blessing and a problem — because if you can’t remember the starting point, you can’t see how far you’ve come. This is the story of an HRT year, and the one small habit that made all of it visible.

A quick note: this is general, educational information, not medical advice. Decisions about HRT, medication and your health should be made with your own prescriber — always follow their guidance and ask your pharmacist if you’re unsure.

The year that blurs

Ask most women how their first HRT year went and you’ll often get a hesitant “better, I think?” That uncertainty isn’t because nothing happened — it’s because change was gradual and memory is short. The women who can tell the story clearly are almost always the ones who wrote a little down along the way.

Month one: the rough start

Picture it: the first weeks are wobbly, a bit of nausea, hope mixed with doubt. Written down, those early notes become a baseline — the thing you’ll later measure everything against. In the moment they feel like nothing. A year on, they’re the evidence that you climbed out of a hole.

Months two to six: settling

Somewhere in here the flushes thin out, sleep knits back together, the fog lifts enough to find your words again. Because it’s gradual, it’s easy to miss day to day. But flick back through a few months of notes and the trend is unmistakable — that’s the moment many women realise it really is working.

The dose that finally fit

Often there’s an adjustment in here — a dose nudged up, a type switched, testosterone added where appropriate. With notes, you can see exactly what each change did. Without them, it’s all guesswork and you risk undoing something that was quietly helping.

The proof, in your own words

The real gift of writing it down isn’t data — it’s the story. At the end of a year you can read, in your own hand, the journey from barely coping to feeling like yourself. On a bad day, that record reminds you how far you’ve come. At a review, it makes every decision sharper.

You can’t go back and start last year’s record. You can start this year’s today — your treatments, supplements, food, movement and how you feel — so next year has a story to tell.

Common questions

What should I track over my first year on HRT?

The few things that matter most to you — sleep, hot flushes, mood, energy — plus your doses and any changes, and lifestyle factors like supplements, food and exercise. The aim is to see the trend over time.

Why keep an HRT diary for a whole year?

Because change is gradual and memory is short. A year of simple notes lets you see how far you’ve come, understand what each dose change did, and make sharper decisions at your reviews.

Is it too late to start tracking if I’m already months into HRT?

Not at all. A baseline from today is far better than none — start now and you’ll still build a clear picture of what’s working from here on.
