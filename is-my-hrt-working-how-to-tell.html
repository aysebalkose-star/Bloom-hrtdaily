Is my HRT working? How to actually tell

It’s the question that loops at 2am: is this actually doing anything? HRT rarely announces itself with fireworks. It works quietly, over weeks, which makes “is it working?” genuinely hard to answer from inside your own tired head. Here’s how to know for real.

A quick note: this is general, educational information, not medical advice. Decisions about HRT, medication and your health should be made with your own prescriber — always follow their guidance and ask your pharmacist if you’re unsure.

Why it’s so hard to tell

Memory is unreliable, especially with perimenopausal brain fog, and we adapt to improvement fast — three weeks of better sleep and you forget how bad it was. Meanwhile one rough night can convince you nothing’s changed. Feelings are real, but they’re a poor measuring tape. You need something steadier.

Start from a baseline

You can only see change from a starting point. If you noted how things were before or as you started — flushes per day, nights you slept through, mood, energy — you have something to compare against. Didn’t capture one? Start today; a baseline from now is still better than none.

Pick a few signals that matter to you

Don’t try to track everything. Choose the three or four things that most affect your life — maybe sleep, hot flushes, mood and energy — and rate them simply, even just 1 to 5, every few days. Specific beats vague: “woke twice” tells you more than “slept badly.”

Read the trend, not the day

After a few weeks, look at the shape, not any single point. Are flushes trending down? Are you waking less? Is the black-dog mood lifting even slightly? A gentle improving line is HRT working, even if yesterday was hard. A flat line after a few months is also an answer — it means it’s worth revisiting your dose or type.

Turn it into a better review

“I think it’s maybe a bit better?” is hard for a clinician to act on. “Flushes down from eight a day to three, but sleep still broken” is gold. When you bring a clear record of your symptoms, doses, supplements and daily life, your review becomes a real conversation about what to adjust — not a guess.

So make it easy on yourself: track the handful of things that matter, in one place, from today. Let the pattern answer the 2am question for you.

Common questions

How do I know if my HRT is working?

Track a few key symptoms — like sleep, hot flushes, mood and energy — regularly and look at the trend over several weeks, rather than judging it on one day. A gentle improvement over time is the sign you’re looking for.

How long before I can tell if HRT is working?

Many people see change over about six to twelve weeks, though it’s often gradual. If symptoms are flat after a few months, that’s useful evidence to discuss adjusting your dose or type with your prescriber.

What if my HRT doesn’t seem to be working?

It may need a dose or type change — that’s common and not a failure. Bring a clear record of your symptoms, doses and daily life to your review so the conversation is based on evidence, not memory.
