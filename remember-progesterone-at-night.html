How to remember your progesterone at night

You climb into bed, turn off the light, and then it lands: did I take the progesterone? Of all the HRT doses, the night-time one is the easiest to lose — you're tired, your routine is loosest, and there's no daytime cue to catch it. Here's how to make it stick, without lying awake wondering.

A quick note: this is general information, not medical advice. How and when to take your progesterone is set by your prescriber — always follow your own prescription and ask your pharmacist if you're unsure.

Why the night dose is the easiest to lose

Micronised progesterone is often taken at bedtime — partly because it can make you a little drowsy, which is no bad thing in menopause. But bedtime is also when your defences are down: the day's structure is gone, you might be reading, scrolling or half-asleep, and there's no "morning coffee" moment to hang the habit on. Forgetting isn't carelessness — it's that the dose lives in the fuzziest part of your day. The fix is to give it a cue of its own.

Anchor it to something you already do at night

The single most reliable trick is to attach the capsule to an action you never skip before bed. Pick one and pair them every night:

  • Brushing your teeth — keep the pack by your toothbrush and take it as you finish.
  • Setting your alarm — one hand on the phone, one on the capsule.
  • Your last glass of water — if you have one on the nightstand, take it then.

Do it in the same order every night and, within a week or two, it stops being something you have to remember at all.

Small tricks that help

  • Move the pack to where you'll see it — on the nightstand or by the bathroom sink, not in a kitchen cupboard.
  • Log it the moment you take it — a single tap or tick. That record is what answers "did I?" when the doubt arrives two minutes later.
  • Take it a touch earlier — with your evening wind-down rather than at lights-out, so you're not relying on a sleepy brain.
  • If it makes you drowsy, that's often the point — but if it leaves you groggy in the morning, mention it at your review; timing can sometimes be adjusted.

If you're lying there unsure you took it

This is the exact moment a log earns its keep — one glance and you know. Without one, don't guess or take a second "just in case": what to do about a missed or doubled dose depends on your specific progesterone, so check the leaflet or ask your pharmacist in the morning. Then set up a cue so tomorrow night has an answer built in.

Common questions

How do I remember to take my progesterone at night?

Anchor it to something you never skip before bed, such as brushing your teeth or setting your alarm, and keep the pack where you will see it like the nightstand. Take it at the same time each night and log it the moment you do, so you can check later rather than lie awake wondering.

Why is progesterone taken at night?

Micronised progesterone is often taken at bedtime partly because it can make you a little drowsy, which can help with sleep. Your prescriber sets the timing for your specific prescription, so always follow their guidance and your product leaflet.

What if I can't remember whether I took my progesterone?

Do not guess or take a second dose to be safe, as what to do about a missed or doubled dose depends on your specific product. Check the leaflet or ask your pharmacist. Keeping a simple log means the next time you wonder, one glance gives you the answer.

Does it matter what time I take progesterone?

Taking it at a consistent time each night helps it become an automatic habit and, for many people, supports sleep. Your prescriber decides the right timing for you, so follow your prescription and mention any morning grogginess at your review.
