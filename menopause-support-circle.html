Building your menopause support circle: GP, friends, work, home

Menopause is not a problem to solve alone in the dark at 3am. It’s a season to move through with people around you. You don’t need one hero who fixes everything — you need a circle, each part carrying a little. Here’s how to build yours.

A quick note: this is general, educational information, not medical advice. Decisions about HRT, medication and your health should be made with your own prescriber — always follow their guidance and ask your pharmacist if you’re unsure.

Why a circle beats a saviour

When we pin all our hopes on one thing — one appointment, one partner, one dose — we set ourselves up to feel let down. A circle spreads the weight. Different people meet different needs, and no single person has to be everything. That’s not a lesser plan; it’s a sturdier one.

Your clinical corner

This is the person (or people) who help with the medical side: a GP with a menopause interest, a pharmacist who answers the small questions without an appointment, maybe a menopause specialist. Their job is easier — and your care better — when you bring a clear history each time.

Your ‘me too’ people

These are the women who say “oh, I had that exact thing.” They normalise what feels frightening and share what actually helped them. One or two trusted friends a few steps ahead can be worth more than any amount of doom-scrolling.

Your home team

Partners, kids old enough to understand, close family. They can’t help with what they don’t know, so let them in a little. Small, specific contributions — a lie-in on a bad night, taking a chore off your plate — add up to feeling held.

Keeping everyone on the same page

A support circle works best when it’s built on the same facts. When you track your treatments, supplements, symptoms and daily life in one place, you can tell your GP what’s really been happening, show your partner it’s real, and notice yourself what’s helping. One clear picture, shared with the people who matter, turns a scattered struggle into a plan.

Common questions

Who should be in my menopause support circle?

A mix: a clinician who takes you seriously, one or two friends who’ve been through it, understanding people at home, and support at work if you need it. You don’t need all of it at once — start with one and build.

I don’t have anyone who understands — where do I start?

Reputable menopause communities and charities are a good first step, and your GP or pharmacist can be part of your circle too. Keeping a record of your own experience also means you’re never relying purely on memory on a hard day.

How do I keep everyone ‘on the same page’?

Keep your treatments, supplements, symptoms and notes in one place so you can share a consistent, accurate picture with your GP, your family and yourself.
