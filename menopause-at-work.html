Menopause at work: what you’re allowed to need

A hot flush in a meeting. Brain fog swallowing the word you need. A sleepless night turning a normal workday into a mountain. Menopause at work is real, and it’s common — and you are allowed to need support, without apology. Here’s what that can look like.

A quick note: this is general, educational information, not medical advice. Decisions about HRT, medication and your health should be made with your own prescriber — always follow their guidance and ask your pharmacist if you’re unsure.

Why work can be the hardest place

Work demands exactly what menopause can disrupt — concentration, memory, energy, calm — and often with the least room to show you’re struggling. Many women quietly reduce hours, turn down promotions, or even leave, not because they can’t do the job, but because no one told them support was possible.

What you’re allowed to ask for

Reasonable adjustments vary by job, but commonly include:

  • a fan, a cooler spot, or control over temperature
  • flexibility on the worst days, or around early meetings when sleep is wrecked
  • breaks, and easier access to water and toilets
  • understanding around brain fog — notes, agendas, a little more time

Many organisations now have menopause policies; it’s worth asking HR what’s available.

Who to tell, and how much

You decide how much to share. Some women tell a trusted manager everything; others simply request a specific adjustment without going into detail. Both are valid. The goal is support, not disclosure for its own sake.

Having the conversation

Framing it around staying effective tends to land well: “I’m managing some health changes and a couple of adjustments would help me keep doing my best work.” Specific, forward-looking, and hard to argue with.

Asking with evidence

It’s easier to ask — and easier to be taken seriously — when you can point to a pattern: “on broken-sleep nights my focus drops; a later start helps.” Keeping a record of your symptoms and what helps turns a nervous, vague ask into a confident, reasonable one.

Track how menopause actually affects your days — symptoms, sleep, what helps — and you’ll walk into that conversation knowing exactly what to ask for, and why.

Common questions

What support can I ask for at work during menopause?

Reasonable adjustments such as a fan or cooler space, flexibility on bad days, breaks, easy access to water and toilets, and understanding around brain fog. Many employers now have a menopause policy — ask HR.

Do I have to tell my employer I’m menopausal?

No — you choose how much to share. You can request a specific adjustment without going into detail, or speak more openly with a trusted manager. Both are valid.

How do I raise menopause with my manager?

Frame it around staying effective and be specific about what would help. Bringing a record of how symptoms affect your work makes the request clearer and easier to grant.
