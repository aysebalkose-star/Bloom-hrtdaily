You're not making it up: real menopause support starts here

There is a particular loneliness in not being believed — sometimes not even by yourself. You know something has shifted, but the blood test came back “normal” and everyone keeps saying you’re just tired. You are not making it up. Here is where real support lives, and how to reach it.

It’s real, and it’s not your fault

Perimenopause can start years before your periods stop, and the hormone swings behind it are wild and uneven. That is why you can feel fine one week and unrecognisable the next. The exhaustion, the sudden tears, the brain that won’t hold a sentence — these are not a character flaw or a sign you’re “not coping”. They are symptoms, and millions of women are living the exact same thing, often in silence.

The first act of support is the one you give yourself: naming it. Not “I’m failing,” but “my body is changing and I need support.” That small reframe is where everything else begins.

Getting medical support that actually listens

You deserve a clinician who takes you seriously. In the UK, perimenopause and menopause are usually diagnosed from your symptoms and age, not a single blood test — so a “normal” result does not mean nothing is wrong. If your GP dismisses you, it is reasonable to ask to see another clinician at the practice who has an interest in menopause, or to ask about a referral. The NHS menopause pages and charities like the Menopause Charity can help you know what good care looks like.

Go in prepared. A clear list of your symptoms, how long they’ve lasted and how much they affect your daily life is far harder to wave away than “I just feel off.”

Finding women who get it

Something shifts the first time another woman says “yes, me too, exactly that.” Peer support — a trusted menopause forum, a local group, a WhatsApp thread with friends a few years ahead of you — does what no leaflet can: it makes you feel sane. Choose spaces that are kind and evidence-aware rather than ones that frighten or sell to you.

Support at home and at work

The people around you can’t support what they don’t understand. A short, honest conversation — “I’m going through perimenopause, here’s what’s hard right now, here’s what would help” — often lands better than you fear. At work, you’re allowed to ask for reasonable adjustments, whether that’s a desk fan, flexibility on the worst days, or simply understanding.

Why a record changes everything

The single most empowering thing you can do is write it down. When you can show a clear picture — these symptoms, this often, this is what helps, this is what I take — you stop being easy to dismiss. You turn “I just feel awful” into evidence. Keep your treatments, supplements, symptoms and daily notes in one place, and your own experience becomes the strongest case you can make for the support you deserve.

Common questions

Is it normal to feel like no one believes me in menopause?

Sadly it’s common — many women report being dismissed, partly because perimenopause is diagnosed from symptoms rather than one clear test. You are not imagining it. Keeping a clear record of your symptoms and how they affect you makes it much easier to be taken seriously.

What kind of menopause support is available?

Medical support from a GP or menopause specialist, peer support from forums and groups of women going through the same thing, and practical support at home and work. Most women benefit from a mix of all three.

How do I get my GP to take my menopause symptoms seriously?

Go in with a written list of your symptoms, how long you’ve had them and how they affect daily life. If you still feel dismissed, it’s reasonable to ask to see another clinician with a menopause interest, or about a referral.
