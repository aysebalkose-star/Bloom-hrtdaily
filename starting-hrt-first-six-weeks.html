Starting HRT: the first six weeks, honestly

You’ve started HRT and now you’re watching yourself like a hawk — is that better? worse? in my head? The beginning is hopeful and confusing in equal measure. Here’s an honest map of the first six weeks, so you know what’s normal and when to hold your nerve.

A quick note: this is general, educational information, not medical advice. Decisions about HRT, medication and your health should be made with your own prescriber — always follow their guidance and ask your pharmacist if you’re unsure.

The hopeful, nervous start

Starting HRT can feel like a small act of hope — and a nerve-wracking one. It’s natural to analyse every feeling. Try to hold it lightly: hormones take time to reach a steady state, and the first days tell you very little on their own. What you’re doing now is gathering a baseline, not grading the treatment.

Early side effects that often settle

Some people notice tender breasts, a little nausea, headaches or spotting in the first weeks as the body adjusts. These are commonly listed as early effects that often ease over the first few months. They can be disheartening if no one warned you — but they are frequently temporary. The NHS HRT pages are a solid place to sense-check what you’re feeling, and your pharmacist can answer the small worries fast.

The wobble around weeks 2–4

This is where many women almost give up. Symptoms can seesaw, and a couple of bad days can feel like proof it isn’t working. Often it’s just the adjustment phase. One hard day is not the trend — which is exactly why you don’t want to be judging it from memory and mood alone.

Starting to settle

For many, things begin to steady over six to twelve weeks — sleep a little deeper, flushes less fierce, mood less jagged. It’s often gradual rather than a switch flipping, which is part of why it’s easy to miss unless you’ve been noting it down. If after a few months things still aren’t right, that’s useful information for your review, not a failure.

Why tracking from day one matters

The kindest thing you can do for the nervous early weeks is write it down. Log your dose each day, note your sleep, mood, flushes and energy, and jot what else you’re doing — supplements, food, movement. When the wobble hits, you can look back and see the real direction of travel instead of trusting a tired brain. That record is also gold at your first review.

Common questions

How long does HRT take to work?

It varies, but many people notice some symptoms easing over the first few weeks, with things often settling over about six to twelve weeks. Give it time and follow your prescriber’s guidance rather than judging it on a single day.

Are side effects normal when starting HRT?

Some people get tender breasts, mild nausea, headaches or spotting early on as the body adjusts, and these often ease over the first months. Check your leaflet, ask your pharmacist, and tell your prescriber if anything is severe or persistent.

Should I stop HRT if I feel worse at first?

Don’t stop or change it on your own — the first few weeks can wobble before they settle. If you’re worried, speak to your prescriber or pharmacist. Keeping a daily record helps you and them see the real trend.
