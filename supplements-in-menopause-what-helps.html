Supplements in menopause: what helps, what’s hype, and how to track it

Walk down the menopause supplement aisle and you’ll be promised the earth — for a price. Some supplements genuinely help some women; many are expensive hope in a bottle. Here’s how to spend wisely, stay safe, and find out what actually works for you.

A quick note: this is general, educational information, not medical advice. Decisions about HRT, medication and your health should be made with your own prescriber — always follow their guidance and ask your pharmacist if you’re unsure.

The wild west of supplements

Supplements aren’t regulated like medicines, so marketing can run far ahead of evidence. A confident label and a glowing testimonial are not proof. That doesn’t mean nothing helps — it means you need a bit of healthy scepticism and a way to tell real benefit from placebo and clever ads.

A sensible way to think

Food first where you can, and be clear what a supplement is actually for. Some, like vitamin D, are widely recommended in certain circumstances — it’s worth asking your GP or pharmacist what’s relevant for you. For the trendier menopause blends, ask: what’s the evidence, and would I notice if it worked?

Staying safe

“Natural” doesn’t mean harmless. Supplements can interact with medicines and each other, and some aren’t suitable with certain conditions or treatments. Before starting anything new — especially alongside HRT or other medication — check with your pharmacist or GP. It’s a free, fast sanity check that can save you money and trouble.

Test one thing at a time

If you start five things at once and feel better, you’ll never know which one did it — or whether it was the better sleep you also prioritised. Change one variable at a time, give it a fair few weeks, and keep everything else steady so the test actually means something.

Track what actually works

This is the part that saves real money. Log what you take, when you started, and the symptoms you’re hoping to shift — then watch for genuine change over the following weeks. If the numbers don’t budge, you can stop with confidence rather than paying for hope. If they do, you’ve found a keeper.

Keep your supplements alongside your treatments, symptoms, food and movement in one place, start one new thing at a time, and let the evidence — not the advert — decide what earns a place in your routine.

Common questions

Do menopause supplements actually work?

Some supplements help some women, but the market is lightly regulated and many claims outrun the evidence. The only way to know if one works for you is to track your symptoms before and after — and change one thing at a time.

Are menopause supplements safe to take with HRT?

Not always — ‘natural’ doesn’t mean risk-free, and supplements can interact with medicines. Check with your pharmacist or GP before starting anything new, especially alongside HRT.

How do I know which supplement is helping?

Start one at a time, keep everything else steady, give it a few weeks, and track the symptoms you’re targeting. If nothing changes you can stop with confidence; if it helps, you’ve found one worth keeping.
