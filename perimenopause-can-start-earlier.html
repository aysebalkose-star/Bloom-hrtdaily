Perimenopause can start earlier than you think

You’re in your early 40s, periods still turning up, but something’s off — sleep, mood, energy, a cycle that’s quietly changing. “Too young for menopause,” you think. Often, you’re not. Perimenopause can start earlier than most of us were ever told.

When it really starts

Perimenopause — the transition before your final period — commonly begins in the mid-40s, but it can start in the early 40s or even the late 30s, and it can last several years. Crucially, it often begins while your periods are still coming, which is exactly why it catches so many women off guard.

The early signs

Early perimenopause tends to whisper before it shouts:

  • cycles that shift — shorter, longer, heavier, lighter, less predictable
  • new or worse PMS, and sleep that’s harder to come by
  • anxiety, irritability or low mood that feels out of character
  • brain fog, dips in energy, or changes in libido

Any one of these is easy to explain away. Together, over time, they often tell a different story.

Why it’s so often missed

Because periods continue, because a one-off blood test can look “normal,” and because the symptoms overlap with busy, stressful midlife, early perimenopause is frequently put down to work, parenting or “just getting older.” Women can spend years not knowing what they’re dealing with.

How tracking reveals it

This is where a record becomes genuinely revealing. Logging your cycle alongside your symptoms often surfaces a pattern — symptoms clustering at certain points, cycles drifting over months — that no single day could show you. Suddenly the scattered complaints have a shape.

What to do with that

Knowing it could be perimenopause, even in your 40s, changes the conversation with your GP from “I’m just tired” to “could this be hormonal?” — backed by evidence. Start tracking your cycle, symptoms, sleep, supplements and daily life now, and you’ll have the pattern in hand when you need it.

Common questions

What age does perimenopause start?

Commonly the mid-40s, but it can begin in the early 40s or even the late 30s, and often while periods are still regular. It can last several years before periods stop.

Can I be in perimenopause if my periods are still regular?

Yes — perimenopause often starts while periods are still coming, with signs like changing cycles, worse PMS, disrupted sleep, mood changes and brain fog. Tracking your cycle and symptoms can reveal the pattern.

Why is early perimenopause so often missed?

Because periods continue, a single blood test can look normal, and symptoms overlap with busy midlife stress. Many women go years without realising — which is why tracking and awareness matter.
