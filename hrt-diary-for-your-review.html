Keeping an HRT diary for your menopause review

You've waited weeks for the appointment, and then you're in the room: "So, how's it going?" — and your mind goes blank. The last few weeks blur into how you feel today, and you leave wishing you'd remembered half of it. A simple HRT diary fixes that — you walk in with the real story, and walk out with a better plan.

A quick note: this is general information, not medical advice. A diary helps you and your prescriber have a better conversation — it doesn't replace their guidance.

Why your memory isn't enough in the room

HRT reviews are often short, and they tend to come weeks or months apart. In that gap, a lot happens — and human memory quietly rewrites it to match how you feel right now. One rough night before the appointment can make a genuinely good month feel like a failure; a good morning can hide weeks of struggle. A diary gives your clinician something memory can't: an honest record of how things actually went, so decisions are based on the pattern, not the mood of the day.

What's worth recording

Keep it light — a diary you dread won't survive the week. A useful entry might note:

  • Your doses — what you took and when, and any you missed.
  • Symptoms — hot flushes, night sweats, sleep, headaches, bleeding, aches.
  • Mood and mind — anxiety, low mood, irritability, brain fog, energy.
  • Anything that changed — a new dose, a different brand, a rough patch and what was going on around it.

You don't need all of it every day. Even a single line most days builds a picture over a few weeks.

How to keep it without it becoming a chore

The best diary is the one that takes seconds and is always with you. A notebook works, but it's easy to leave at home; a dedicated tracker lives on your phone, logs a dose in a tap, and keeps the record for you. Whatever you choose, make it tiny and attach it to something you already do — a line with your evening cup of tea, a tick when you take a dose. Consistency over months beats detail for a week.

Turning your diary into appointment notes

A day or two before your review, read back over the weeks and jot three things: what's clearly better, what's still bothering you, and any questions you want answered. That short summary — backed by the record behind it — is pure gold to a clinician. Instead of "I think it's maybe a bit better?", you can say "sweats down, sleep up, but mood still low three weeks in" — and get a real adjustment rather than a guess.

Common questions

What should I write in an HRT diary?

Keep it light, a line most days is plenty. Note your doses and any you missed, your main symptoms such as flushes, sleep and bleeding, your mood and energy, and anything that changed like a new dose or a rough patch. Over a few weeks this builds an honest picture for your review.

Why keep a diary before a menopause review?

Reviews are short and weeks apart, and memory quietly rewrites how the time went to match how you feel today. A diary gives your clinician the real pattern rather than the mood of the day, so decisions and dose adjustments are based on what actually happened.

How do I prepare for an HRT review appointment?

A day or two before, read back over your diary and jot three things: what is clearly better, what still bothers you, and any questions. That short summary, backed by your record, lets you say something specific like sweats down, sleep up, mood still low, and get a real adjustment.

Do I need an app to keep an HRT diary?

No, a notebook works, though it is easy to leave at home. An app can be handy because it is always on your phone, logs a dose in a tap, and keeps the record for you, so your appointment summary is ready when you need it.
