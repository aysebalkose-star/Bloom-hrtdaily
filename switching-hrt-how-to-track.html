Switching HRT? Here's how to track the change

A new dose, a different brand, patches instead of tablets — switching your HRT is common, and often exactly the right move. But it can leave you wondering: is this actually better, or do I just want it to be? The way to answer honestly is to track the change, so you can see the difference instead of guessing at it.

A quick note: this is general information, not medical advice. Any change to your HRT should be made with your prescriber — this is about keeping a clear record while you do.

Why a switch is hard to judge from the inside

When you change something, you want to know quickly whether it worked — but HRT rarely answers quickly. Benefits arrive gradually, your mood colours how you read each day, and a single bad night can convince you the new regimen is worse when the trend is actually up. Without a record, you're comparing a hazy memory of "before" with how you feel this exact morning. That's not a fair test — and it's how good changes get abandoned too soon.

Note where you're starting from

Before the switch, or right as it happens, capture a quick baseline: how your sleep, flushes, mood and energy are right now, and what you're taking. It takes two minutes and gives you something concrete to compare against later. Think of it as a "before" photo for how you feel — easy to dismiss in the moment, invaluable a month on.

What to track through the change

  • The new regimen — what you're now taking, and from which date.
  • Your main symptoms — the ones that made you switch, tracked the same way as before.
  • Mood, sleep and energy — often the clearest signals of whether it's heading the right way.
  • Any side effects — new or settling, so you can tell your prescriber precisely.

Keep it to a quick daily line. After a few weeks you can lay the "after" beside the "before" and actually see the direction of travel.

Give it time to settle

Your body needs a stretch to adjust to a new dose or delivery method — some effects shift within weeks, while the fuller picture can take up to around three months. Early wobbles don't necessarily mean the switch failed. A record keeps you from judging too soon, and if things genuinely aren't improving, it gives your prescriber the detail to fine-tune again rather than start from scratch.

Common questions

How do I know if a new HRT dose is working better?

Capture a quick baseline of your sleep, flushes, mood and energy before or as you switch, then track the same things afterwards in a daily line. After a few weeks you can lay the after beside the before and see the direction of travel, rather than comparing a hazy memory with how you feel today.

How long does it take to adjust to a new HRT dose?

Some effects shift within a few weeks, while the fuller picture can take up to around three months as your body settles. Early wobbles do not necessarily mean the switch failed, which is why a record helps you avoid judging too soon.

What should I track when switching HRT?

Note the new regimen and the date it started, the symptoms that made you switch, your mood, sleep and energy, and any side effects that are new or settling. Keep it to a quick daily line so you can compare clearly and give your prescriber precise detail.

Should I stop a new HRT if I feel worse at first?

Talk to your prescriber rather than stopping on your own, as early adjustment can involve some ups and downs. A record of exactly what you are experiencing helps them decide whether to wait, tweak the dose, or try another option.
